# Date Scenario Voice Reference

Three short, performed comedic date scenarios, kept as a voice and tone reference for spoken date dialogue: one date that goes well, one that crashes out, and a run of rapid-fire getting-to-know-you bits. The value here is response dynamics, the way two people across a table actually answer each other: compression, mishearing, ignored setup, abrupt sincerity, teasing, escalating absurdity, concrete asks, and the way a single bad beat can spiral the whole exchange. Useful when a member should sound like a person reacting in real time instead of a profile reciting a checklist.

Speakers are labeled `A` and `B`, reset per scene, with `A` speaking first. Time-skips inside a scene are marked `_(later)_`. Quick bits are titled by their opening question. Light copy-editing normalizes capitalization and punctuation for readability, but intentional performance markers are preserved, because those are the reference: vowel elongation ("Nooooope"), raised volume in all-caps ("WHO?!", "PLEASE! CLOSE YOUR EYES"), breathless run-ons, deliberate mishearings and dodged answers, and non-verbal sound gags kept as parentheticals. Names spoken in the dialogue are character lines, not stable identities; the crash-out scene even argues about one (Natasha vs Natalie).

## Scene 1: The date that goes well

A warm read. The well-adjusted partner keeps answering, keeps asking, and refuses to let a self-conscious spiral land. Sincerity arrives plainly and is met plainly.

A: Hey!

B: Hi, Gregory, right?

A: Greg, actually.

B: What?

A: I said I prefer Greg, actually.

B: Oh, sorry, yeah, alright.

A: And you're Riley?

B: Yeah.

A: Really been looking forward to meeting you. There aren't that many people on dating apps that I actually enjoy talking to, you know?

B: Yeah, same.

_(later)_

A: I'm a pediatrician, but I donate most of the money to shelters for pets that are displaced by the wildfire.

B: Oh my god, that's incredible.

A: And you were saying you work for a podcast company, right?

B: Network. Startup. Yeah, we're on the up and up though, just as soon as we deal with some finance stuff.

A: That's awesome, dude.

B: What did you say?

A: I said, do you like it?

B: Oh yeah, I do, but it's just... um, there's this one co-worker, and he's, well. I don't really know how else to say this, but he's a dumbass.

A: Oh.

B: He's not, like, totally dumb. Like, he's aware of what he's doing, and somehow that makes it worse.

B: It's like we can't go through one day at the office without him pitching some dumb scheme, or, like, bringing in some Florida guy from the toilet.

A: What?

B: You had to have been there.

B: Like, he makes me look bad. Like, I love my job and he makes it bad for me.

B: Oh my god, I'm so embarrassed, I'm so sorry, this was supposed to be a fun night and you're such a catch and I'm just messing it all up and I'm ruining everything.

A: No, no, you're not ruining anything at all.

A: This just points to how passionate and driven and smart you are, and it seems like you're really just trying to help this guy be the best version of himself that he can be.

A: I think that's an admirable quality.

B: What?

A: I hope this isn't too forward, but I would love to take you home.

B: YES.

## Scene 2: The date that crashes out

The opposite engine. One side keeps committing harder to a bit nobody agreed to, escalating from odd to hostile, while the other side narrates her own exit in real time. Note how the absurdity is procedural, not random: each line builds on the last bad premise.

A: Black bean burger on a bed of rice...

B: Hi! Hi.

A: Oh! I'm so sorry I'm late.

B: No, no, I'm sorry I'm late. Traffic was horrendous.

A: Well, I'm gonna agree to disagree.

_(later)_

B: So what do you do?

A: Oh, I own a podcast company.

B: Oh wow, really? That's so cool, I love podcasts! Which network?

A: Earwolf.

B: Doesn't Scott Aukerman own Earwolf?

A: Okay... okay, so you do know some shit.

B: Yeah. Wait, what are you saying, that I don't know any shit?

A: Captain! We'll have two brandies.

B: I would love... not that. Just a red wine, please.

A: Pinot Noir and a brandy!

A: And can you do me a favor? Can you step on it? No rush, but just really be sure to crush the glass under your heel.

A: So, have you seen Mr. Magorium's Wonder Emporium?

B: Why?

A: Well, you remind me of the titular character, if I'm being honest.

B: Mr. Magorium?

A: WHO?!

_(later)_

B: What are you doing?

A: Well, I have a rule of thumb that if the food takes longer than five minutes, you know, I have to have a take five.

B: You HAVE to have a take five?

A: Let me ask you, do you trust me?

B: We barely know each other.

A: Natasha, please, we know each other.

B: My name is Natalie.

A: Close your eyes.

B: Absolutely not.

A: PLEASE! CLOSE YOUR EYES.

B: Okay, okay, please stop yelling, ok.

A: Now I want you to picture a bird so plump, you could call it Fowler.

A: ...The waiter was supposed to kind of bring your chicken right then. It was gonna be, like, perfectly timed. We talked about it before you got here.

B: I'm gonna go... to the ladies' room.

A: Oh, well, I can watch your purse.

B: No, no, I think I'm just gonna bring my purse with me.

## Quick bits: getting to know each other

Rapid back-and-forth, one premise each. A asks a flat getting-to-know-you question; B answers in a way that derails it. The comedy is in the immediate non-answer, the slip covered too late, and the deadpan escalation, with no laugh-tag to cushion it.

### "When was your last serious relationship?"

A: When was your last serious relationship?

B: Pass.

A: Jeff, you can't pass, this is a basic question.

B: That's WAY too personal.

A: You said you're applying to a reality dating show. They're gonna be pretty invasive the whole time!

B: THEN I DON'T WANNA DO IT.

### "What are some of your best qualities?"

A: What are some of your best qualities?

B: I try to keep it tight, I try to keep it right, and I try to keep it white.

A: What?

B: I try to keep it a hunnid, is what I was saying.

A: You said white.

### "Why did your last relationship end?"

A: Why did your last relationship end?

B: She said I wasn't a good listener. And I sliced her dad.

A: You what?

B: I sort of... (slicing noise)

### "Do you want a pet?"

A: Do you want a pet?

B: Yes! Three vultures, all of them circling.

A: I think I'm looking for more like a cat or dog.

B: I think I'm looking for someone else.

### "Are you a flirt?"

A: Are you a flirt?

B: Nope.

A: Are you a generous lover?

B: Nooooope.

A: Would you say you're a good partner?

B: This is tough...

A: Shouldn't be...
